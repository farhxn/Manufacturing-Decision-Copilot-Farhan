# Intended Users, Assumptions, Limitations, and Human-Approval Points
## Manufacturing Decision Copilot

---

## 1. Intended Users

### Primary User — Procurement Manager

**Who they are:** The person accountable for selecting and qualifying suppliers for a manufacturing component. They own the sourcing decision and must justify it to engineering, finance, and senior leadership.

**What they need from this system:**
- A ranked list of suppliers with a clear, defensible recommendation
- Evidence they can point to when questioned ("the system cited this on page 3 of the quotation")
- A fast way to test "what if shipping costs rise" without rebuilding a spreadsheet
- An executive-ready output they can share without re-writing everything from scratch

**How they interact with it:**
- Upload supplier quotations and certificates to the Documents page
- Review the dashboard recommendation card with score breakdown and citations
- Run scenario simulations (shipping shock, tariff change) via the Scenario Simulator
- Generate and download an executive PDF report for stakeholder distribution

**Success criteria for this user:** They can make a sourcing recommendation to leadership in under 30 minutes, with every data point traceable to a source document.

---

### Secondary User — Manufacturing / Quality Engineer

**Who they are:** The technical reviewer responsible for confirming a supplier can meet the component's dimensional tolerances, material specs, and quality standards.

**What they need from this system:**
- Verification that required certifications (ISO 9001, AS9100D, IATF 16949) are present and valid
- Visibility into defect rates, inspection pass rates, and quality scores per supplier
- The capability matching output (does this supplier actually do CNC machining to the required spec?)
- Compliance score = 0 flags to catch disqualifying gaps before issuing a purchase order

**How they interact with it:**
- Supplier detail page (`/suppliers/{id}`) — risk breakdown, certification list, capability match
- Evidence panel — click any quality metric to see the supporting document excerpt
- Comparison view — side-by-side quality and compliance dimensions for shortlisted suppliers

**Success criteria for this user:** They can confirm or challenge a supplier qualification decision in one session without hunting through stacked PDFs.

---

### Secondary User — Operations / Supply Chain Manager

**Who they are:** Responsible for supply continuity, lead time commitments, and managing disruption risk across the supplier base.

**What they need from this system:**
- Delivery scores and lead time rankings across all suppliers
- Risk breakdown: which suppliers have high country risk, supply chain risk, or capacity constraints?
- Scenario simulation: what happens to the ranking if a major shipping lane is disrupted?
- Identification of single-source dependencies and near-shore alternatives

**How they interact with it:**
- Dashboard supplier landscape table — delivery and risk columns
- Scenario Simulator — shipping multiplier and lead time adjustment sliders
- Risk breakdown card — per-factor risk with primary driver identification

**Success criteria for this user:** They can identify the most supply-chain-resilient shortlist in a single dashboard session.

---

### Tertiary User — Executive / Finance Leadership

**Who they are:** The audience for the sourcing decision, not the analyst. They need a concise, confident summary they can approve in a 10-minute review.

**What they need from this system:**
- A one-page board-ready recommendation with no jargon
- Cost savings quantified in dollars, not percentages
- Top 3 risks stated plainly
- A clear "recommended next step"

**How they interact with it:**
- Receive the generated PDF executive report — they do not log into the platform
- The report is generated by ExecutiveSummaryAgent and includes: recommendation statement, key findings, risk summary, cost comparison, and next steps

**Success criteria for this user:** They can approve or challenge the recommendation within the meeting without requesting additional analysis.

## 2. Assumptions

### 2.1 Data Quality Assumptions

| Assumption | Implication if violated |
|---|---|
| Uploaded documents contain text-layer PDFs (not scanned images) | OCR fallback via pytesseract activates; extraction quality drops; confidence score decreases |
| Supplier quotations use standard commercial fields (unit price, lead time, Incoterms, certifications) | ExtractionAgent may leave fields null; missing fields reduce `data_completeness` in confidence formula |
| Duty rates in documents reflect the actual applicable tariff for the buyer's country of import | If incorrect, landed cost calculations will be wrong; the system has no way to verify duty rates independently |
| Certificate `is_valid` flags are set correctly during data ingestion | An incorrectly marked `is_valid=True` on an expired cert will allow a non-compliant supplier to pass the compliance check |
| Risk scores in `supplier_risk_scores` are populated for all 5 factors per supplier | Missing risk sub-scores default to safety score 70 (moderate); the system logs a `"default"` data_source warning but does not fail |
| All 10 suppliers are evaluated against the same component specification | Comparing suppliers quoting different scopes of work will produce misleading relative scores |

### 2.2 Scoring Model Assumptions

| Assumption | Basis |
|---|---|
| Default weight distribution (cost 30%, quality 20%, delivery 15%, risk 15%, capability 10%, compliance 10%) reflects a balanced manufacturing procurement context | Derived from procurement literature and the challenge pack specification; configurable per project |
| Landed cost is the correct cost basis for comparison (not unit price alone) | Industry standard; total cost of ownership begins with landed cost |
| A missing mandatory certification is a hard disqualifier (compliance_score = 0), not a partial penalty | Conservative choice aligned with quality management system requirements (ISO 9001, AS9100D requirements explicitly state approved supplier status is required) |
| BM25 + cosine vector + RRF is sufficient for retrieval quality given the domain vocabulary of manufacturing documents | Validated against the 17-document challenge pack; domain terms (ISO 9001, IATF 16949, FOB, lead time, ppm) retrieve correctly |
| The Confidence Engine's 5-factor formula (extraction 30%, evidence 20%, retrieval 20%, rule agreement 20%, data completeness 10%) is a reasonable proxy for recommendation reliability | Based on the roadmap specification; not calibrated against a labelled ground-truth dataset of procurement decisions |

### 2.3 AI Behaviour Assumptions

| Assumption | Implication |
|---|---|
| The LLM (Gemini 2.0 Flash / GPT-4o) produces factually grounded output when given sanitised, structured evidence | Validated by schema enforcement + evidence ID filtering; hallucinated references are stripped before storage |
| 7 injection pattern types cover the realistic attack surface for manufacturing document injection | Coverage validated by 24 unit tests in `test_phase6_edge_cases.py`; novel patterns not in the catalogue may pass through |
| LLM explanations are approximately stable across retries for the same inputs | Minor phrasing variation is acceptable; quantitative claims come from the deterministic engines, not the LLM |
| A 10-second timeout is sufficient for LLM calls under normal API conditions | Redis cache fallback activates on timeout; no recommendation is lost |

## 3. Limitations

### 3.1 Data Limitations

**No live external data:**  
The system does not query live commodity prices, exchange rates, sanctions lists, credit rating services, or supplier financial databases. All risk scores are either extracted from uploaded documents or entered manually via the seed script. The system explicitly logs "default" data source when a risk factor is inferred rather than document-backed.

**Currency rates are static:**  
The `currency_rate` field in `SupplierInput` is set at evaluation time from document data or scenario config. The system does not fetch live FX rates. A quotation denominated in EUR made when EUR/USD = 1.08 will not automatically update if the rate moves to 1.12 by the time the purchase order is issued.

**Certification validity is binary:**  
The compliance engine checks whether a certification is held (`is_valid=True`) — it does not check certificate scope, revision level, or whether the specific manufacturing site is covered. A supplier may hold ISO 9001 for one facility but quote from another.

**MOQ is stored but not enforced in scoring:**  
SteelPath Industries has MOQ = 2,000 units. The scoring engines do not reduce a supplier's score because its MOQ exceeds the buyer's order volume. This is surfaced as a `limitations` item in the AI recommendation output but does not affect the numeric ranking.

**10-supplier dataset:**  
The demo dataset is intentionally small (10 suppliers) to make the evaluation tractable and results explainable. Production use would involve larger supplier pools; no re-architecture is required, but retrieval latency in ChromaDB will scale with corpus size.

### 3.2 AI Limitations

**LLM explanations are not legally binding advice:**  
The AI-generated recommendation narrative, executive summary, and risk explanation are decision support outputs. They are not procurement contracts, legal opinions, regulatory rulings, or engineering certifications. Every AI output includes a standard disclaimer:
> "This analysis is AI-assisted decision support. Legal, regulatory, and engineering advice requires human expert verification."

**Extraction accuracy degrades on non-standard documents:**  
Supplier quotations with non-standard layouts, tables embedded in images, or multi-language content will produce lower extraction confidence. The confidence score will reflect this (lower `extraction_quality` input).

**The LLM cannot read documents it was not given:**  
The system only retrieves and reasons over documents that have been uploaded and processed. If a supplier has submitted relevant certifications or updated pricing outside the system, the recommendation will not reflect it.

**Confidence score is not probabilistic:**  
The 5-factor confidence formula is a deterministic weighted composite, not a calibrated probability. A confidence of 87.6% does not mean there is an 87.6% probability the recommendation is correct — it means the evidence completeness and internal consistency are strong. Calibration against actual procurement outcomes has not been performed.

**Self-RAG loop is bounded at 3 iterations:**  
The retrieval loop will stop after 3 passes regardless of coverage score. For very sparse or domain-unusual documents, the evidence may still be incomplete after 3 loops. The system returns the best available evidence set and logs `stopped_reason: "max_loops"`.

### 3.3 Operational Limitations

**Celery + Redis required for document processing:**  
The async document pipeline requires a running Celery worker and Redis instance. In environments where Redis is unavailable, document uploads will queue but not process. The scoring engines and dashboard work without Celery if the database is already seeded.

**No multi-tenancy or authentication in demo configuration:**  
The demo uses a single hardcoded organization and project UUID. The architecture supports multi-tenant operation (organizations → projects → suppliers hierarchy exists in the data model) but user authentication and access control are not enforced in the hackathon prototype.

**Report PDF generation requires the frontend:**  
Executive report PDFs are rendered client-side using `@react-pdf/renderer`. A backend-only deployment cannot produce PDFs without a headless browser or a backend PDF library — this is a known architectural gap for server-side report generation.

## 4. Human-Approval Points

The system is designed around the principle that **the AI recommends, humans approve**. The following points in the workflow require or strongly recommend human review before acting on a system output.

### HAP-1 — Supplier Onboarding and Qualification

**Trigger:** A new supplier appears in the ranking for the first time.  
**Why human review is required:** The system evaluates suppliers on the data it has. It cannot perform physical audits, verify financial statements independently, check for sanctions exposure, or confirm manufacturing site quality in person.  
**What the human must do:** Verify the supplier's legal status, conduct a site audit or third-party audit (evidenced by the NovaCast audit report in the demo dataset), and confirm the qualification meets the buyer's internal supplier approval procedure.  
**System support:** The NovaCast Supplier Audit Report is ingested and retrievable as evidence. The system will cite it. It does not replace the audit.

---

### HAP-2 — Compliance Score = 0 (Auto-Disqualification)

**Trigger:** Any supplier receives compliance_score = 0 (missing or expired required certification).  
**Why human review is required:** The system disqualifies on the basis of the data it holds. A supplier may have renewed its certification after the document upload date, or may hold a valid certificate not yet uploaded.  
**What the human must do:** Confirm with the supplier whether the certificate has been renewed and request an updated certificate document. The system provides the specific expired certificate name, issuer, and expiry date to make this follow-up targeted.  
**System support:** Scenario `certification_overrides` allows the team to simulate what the ranking would look like if the cert were valid — helping prioritize which disqualified suppliers are worth following up with.

---

### HAP-3 — Confidence Score Below 60% (Low Confidence)

**Trigger:** The confidence engine returns a score below 0.60 (label: "Low").  
**Why human review is required:** A low confidence score means one or more of: extraction quality was poor (missing fields), evidence coverage was thin (few relevant chunks retrieved), rule agreement was weak (scores conflict across dimensions), or data completeness was low (risk sub-scores missing).  
**What the human must do:** Review the confidence breakdown card to identify which factor is low. If `extraction_quality` is the driver: re-check the uploaded documents. If `evidence_coverage` is low: upload additional supplier documents. If `data_completeness` is low: manually enter missing risk scores for the affected supplier.  
**System support:** The dashboard confidence breakdown card shows per-factor contributions. The recommendation's `limitations` field in the AI output will name the specific gaps.

---

### HAP-4 — Narrow Margin Between Top Two Suppliers (< 2 points)

**Trigger:** The score gap between rank #1 and rank #2 is less than 2.0 points.  
**Why human review is required:** A 1–2 point gap is within the margin of plausible data variation. A single field value being slightly different (e.g., the supplier's actual defect rate is 3.0% not 2.5%) could reverse the ranking. The mathematical winner may not be the operationally correct choice.  
**What the human must do:** Review the head-to-head comparison view. Identify the specific dimensions driving the gap. Consider whether any dimension should carry more weight given the specific project requirements (e.g., delivery criticality for a JIT production line). Consider running a weight-sensitivity scenario.  
**System support:** The dashboard sensitivity banner appears automatically when the margin is below threshold. The `ScenarioSimulator` weight sliders allow real-time exploration of how the gap changes as weights shift.  
**Demo example:** FastTrack (93.37) vs Acme (91.89) — 1.48-point gap triggers this point. The AS9100D hidden disqualifier was identified by human review enabled by the system's explicit conflict surfacing (Case 2 in the demonstrations document).

---

### HAP-5 — Scenario-Driven Winner Change

**Trigger:** A scenario simulation produces a different #1 supplier than the baseline.  
**Why human review is required:** A scenario represents a hypothetical future state. The new #1 supplier is not automatically the correct choice — it is the correct choice *if* the scenario conditions materialise. The human must assess the likelihood and timing of those conditions.  
**What the human must do:** Review the scenario explanation (ScenarioExplainerAgent output). Assess whether the scenario conditions (e.g., shipping +40%) are plausible in the procurement timeline. Decide whether to hedge by dual-sourcing or to commit to the scenario-adjusted winner.  
**System support:** The ScenarioExplainerAgent provides `key_drivers`, `winner_rationale`, `loser_rationale`, and `caveats` in plain English alongside the before/after ranking delta.

---

### HAP-6 — Executive Report Before Distribution

**Trigger:** Before the generated PDF report is sent to leadership or external stakeholders.  
**Why human review is required:** The executive summary is AI-generated. It may contain phrasing that misrepresents internal context, regulatory nuance, or commercial sensitivities not captured in the uploaded documents.  
**What the human must do:** Read the generated report in full before distributing. Verify that the recommendation statement, cost figures, and risk bullets are accurate relative to the team's understanding of the situation. The report includes a standard AI disclaimer; this does not substitute for the procurement manager's sign-off.  
**System support:** The report is generated on demand and can be regenerated after any document update or scenario change. It is not automatically sent to anyone.

---

### HAP-7 — Final Supplier Selection and Purchase Order Issuance

**Trigger:** At the moment of issuing a purchase order, supplier contract, or formal letter of intent.  
**Why human review is required:** The system is explicitly scoped as decision *support*, not decision *authority*. It never approves suppliers, sends emails, places orders, or negotiates contracts. The procurement manager is fully accountable for the decision.  
**What the human must do:** Apply their professional judgment, internal approval process, and any organizational policies (e.g., preferred supplier lists, ethics screening, ESG commitments) before issuing any binding commercial instrument.  
**System boundary:** The Manufacturing Decision Copilot terminates at "recommendation." Everything after that is a human action.

## 5. What the System Will Not Do

These are hard boundaries — not missing features, but deliberate design decisions:

| Prohibited Action | Reason |
|---|---|
| Contact suppliers directly (email, API, portal) | Would constitute unauthorized commercial activity on behalf of the organization |
| Approve or create purchase orders | Transactional authority belongs to humans; system is advisory only |
| Fetch live supplier financial data, credit scores, or sanctions status | Requires authenticated access to third-party services not within scope; results would need legal review |
| Provide legal, customs, or engineering advice | Requires licensed professional judgment; AI outputs in these domains must be treated as starting points for expert review |
| Scrape supplier websites for updated pricing | Off-limits per system guardrails; all pricing comes from uploaded documents |
| Make decisions when confidence is Low without flagging | The system never silently presents a low-confidence recommendation as authoritative |
| Run without displaying the AI disclaimer | Every AI-generated output surface includes the standard disclaimer |
